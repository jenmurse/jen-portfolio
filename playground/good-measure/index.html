Why

Calorie apps are built for packaged food and barcode scanners. If you cook from scratch, there's no good way to see the nutrition of what you actually make, or to plan a week of real meals against your goals. Good Measure is for that, with a shared pantry, recipes with live nutrition, and a weekly plan for each person in the house against their own targets.

How it works

Six moments from a week of planning, in the order you would use it. Start from the day at a glance, build the week, and dial in a recipe. Then let the numbers fix a day, save it as a template, and turn the week into a shopping list.

How it was made

I designed and built it with the help of Claude Code, starting from my own kitchen, where my husband and I cook from the same pantry with different goals. Every number comes from the pantry. Each ingredient carries its nutrition to the gram, so recipes and days add up from real food. When a day misses its targets, the optimizer looks through your recipes for swaps that fit each meal, a breakfast for a breakfast, and suggests the ones that bring the day closest to your goals.

The dedicated MCP takes it further. Connect the AI assistant you already use, like Claude or ChatGPT, and you can ask for things in plain language. It reads your recipes and plans, and writes changes straight back, whether that's a lighter version of a recipe or a whole week of dinners.

Good Measure is invite-only, for my friends and family.
