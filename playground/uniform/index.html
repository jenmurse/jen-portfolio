Uniform

A closet app for wearing what you own and buying what you'll wear

For
Myself
Role
Design and build
Development

iOS app

The idea

I save a lot of outfit inspiration, and my closet never quite catches up to it. The looks I save drift one way and the clothes I buy drift another, and the result is a closet full of things that felt right in the store and never get worn. Closet apps catalogue what you own. Inspiration apps collect what you like. Shopping apps want you to buy more. None of them meets you at the moment that matters, which is right before you buy. Uniform is built for that moment, and its promise runs the other way from a shopping app: stop buying clothes you'll never wear.

How it works

  1. 01Add a piece

    One photo of something you own, even a quick one on the bed. The background comes off and the type and colours fill themselves in, so the closet builds as you go.

  2. 02Save a look

    Inspiration comes in from wherever you save it. Each look is broken into the pieces in it, so the app knows the wide-leg trousers and the jacket, not just a photo.

  3. 03What's missing

    Across everything you save, the pieces that keep coming back that you don't own, or own too few of. Every gap shows the looks behind it.

  4. 04Dress from a look

    Pick a saved look and get outfits built only from clothes you already own, matched to its palette.

  5. 05The fitting room

    Before you buy something, photograph it or paste its link. You get a verdict, the saved looks it's reaching toward, the closest things you own, and what you'd wear it with.

  6. 06Consider letting go

    The other half of the promise: the pieces that sit furthest from the taste you keep saving, and the ones that never make it into an outfit.

Decisions

Your taste, not the app's. Every verdict is measured against your own saved looks and closet, not a stylist's idea of good.

Never ask for the whole closet. One photo does the work and pieces arrive as you go, because cataloguing is where closet apps lose people.

The verdict counts what you own. Owning one black sweater isn't a reason not to buy a second if they run through a third of the looks you save. Owning five is.

Two worlds you can tell apart. What you own always appears as a cutout on pale grey, and what inspires you always appears as a full photograph.

How it was made

I designed and built it with Claude Code as a one-shot, to see how well Fable could execute my detailed vision. I then refined it against my own closet and the looks I'd saved. Everything runs on the phone. Small image models cut each piece out, name it and read its colours, then compare it with your closet and your saved looks, so nothing leaves the device and there's no account.
